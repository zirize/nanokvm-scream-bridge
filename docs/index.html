Hear your phone on your PC

Plug the phone into a NanoKVM. The phone sees wired headphones; the NanoKVM sends every sample, unchanged, over the LAN to a Scream receiver on your PC.

Repository on GitHub How it works

This is for you if:

  • You want a phone's sound — a game, a video, a music app — to come out of your PC's speakers or headset, and you already listen to everything else there.
  • Bluetooth to the PC adds latency and re-encodes the audio with a lossy codec, and the PC may not even have Bluetooth.
  • A cable from the headphone jack into the PC's line-in hums, needs a free input, and most phones no longer have the jack.
  • You already run Scream for a virtual machine or another computer and would like the phone to be one more Scream source.
  • You have a NanoKVM sitting idle.

What it does

phone ──USB-C (UAC1 speaker)──▶ NanoKVM ──Scream UDP, wired LAN──▶ PC receiver ──▶ speakers

The NanoKVM's USB-C port is normally a USB device — keyboard, mouse and virtual disk for the computer it controls. The kernel can also present that port as a USB Audio Class 1 speaker. Android accepts it as wired headphones and routes all audio there.

On the NanoKVM, arecord reads what the phone plays (48 kHz, 16-bit, stereo) and a 40-line Python script wraps each 288 frames in a Scream header and sends it as one UDP packet. Any Scream receiver plays it: the Unix receiver from duncanthrax/scream, or pipewire-scream.

Nothing is resampled or re-encoded on the way. A 50-second capture produced 166.7 packets per second — exactly 48 kHz — and the sample values reach the PC as the phone produced them.

Setup

You need a NanoKVM reachable as root@<host> over ssh with a key, a Scream receiver on the PC listening on a UDP port (4015 below) that the firewall lets in from the LAN, and a USB-C cable from the phone to the NanoKVM.

git clone https://github.com/zirize/nanokvm-scream-bridge
cd nanokvm-scream-bridge
tools/install-nanokvm.sh <host>                    # copy two files to the device, once
KVM_HOST=<host> tools/kvm-mode audio <pc-ip>:4015  # switch to audio mode
KVM_HOST=<host> tools/kvm-mode status
KVM_HOST=<host> tools/kvm-mode kvm                  # back to the normal KVM

The mode switch takes effect immediately and survives a reboot: the destination is stored in /boot/usb.audio. To recover without the network, put the SD card in a computer and delete that file.

Audio mode replaces the KVM functions. The NanoKVM's USB controller has 8 endpoints and the stock gadget (three HID devices, virtual disk, RNDIS) already uses all of them, so the speaker cannot be added beside them. While in audio mode the NanoKVM is not a KVM.

How it stays up

An init script, S04usbaudio, runs after the firmware's own S03usbdev (which it never modifies). It rebuilds the gadget as a single UAC1 function and starts a supervisor that

  • restarts the capture pipe whenever it ends — phone unplugged, card gone after a rebind;
  • checks every 5 seconds whether the NanoKVM web app has rebuilt the stock gadget (it does when you toggle virtual disk or network settings) and switches it back to audio.

Re-applying audio mode while it is already active does not re-enumerate the phone, so playback is not interrupted.

Things worth knowing

  • Keep the phone at full volume. The phone scales the samples digitally; at low volume the stream peaked at −50 dBFS. Set loudness on the PC.
  • When the gadget re-enumerates (reboot, mode switch) Android treats it as headphones being unplugged and pauses playback. Press play again.
  • UAC1 on this kernel has no feedback endpoint: the phone's clock drives the stream and the receiver absorbs any drift.
  • A phone on the USB-C port keeps the NanoKVM powered. To power-cycle it, remove both the phone and the power supply.
  • The stock shutdown can hang in the NanoKVM's own service stop. Remount / and /boot read-only over ssh before pulling power.

Other boards: Pi 4, Pi Zero 2 W, Pico W

Nothing here depends on the NanoKVM except the gadget setup. Any board with a USB device port can be the speaker. These are worked out from the hardware, not built yet:

NanoKVM (tested)Ethernetplain USB-C cablethis repository
Raspberry Pi 4EthernetUSB-C power/data splitterconfigfs gadget + the same uac2scream.py
Pi Zero 2 WWi-FiOTG cable to the data portsame as the Pi 4
Pico W / Pico 2 WWi-FiOTG cable; the phone powers itnew firmware: TinyUSB UAC2 + UDP

Pi 4: the USB-C port is the only one that can be a gadget, and it is also the power input. A phone cannot power a Pi 4, and two 5 V sources on one rail is asking for trouble, so a USB-C power/data splitter sits in between: the Pi's supply goes to one side, the phone's data to the other. If the splitter's data port has no CC line, an OTG 2-in-1 adapter on the phone provides it and lets a charger keep the phone topped up at the same time.

Pico W: no Linux, so it needs firmware of its own, but the parts exist: TinyUSB has UAC2 speaker examples (one with an asynchronous feedback endpoint), and the Scream stream is only about 1.5 Mbit/s of UDP over Wi-Fi. It draws so little that the phone can power it directly. Full details are in the hardware notes.

Why not a USB-to-USB box

The first idea was a box that is a USB speaker to the phone and a USB microphone to the PC. That needs two USB device controllers, and the NanoKVM and the Raspberry Pi 4 have one each. Two RP2040 boards linked by I2S, or an STM32 with two OTG cores, could do it bit-perfectly. Sending Scream to the PC needs only one controller and reuses receivers that already exist. The hardware notes cover that choice.

Tested on

A full NanoKVM (hardware beta, SG2002 RISC-V, firmware kernel 5.10.4) with one Android phone, sending to the Unix Scream receiver on a Linux PC. MIT licensed.
